### Q1. The k bracket

- **k is not a bracket. It is exactly 1.6, so k 0.8 is not a possible worst end.** `frame_topopt.assemble_loads` multiplies the *whole* set by the case factor (`F = factor x f`), the unscaled cinch loop included. `frame_comp_d.evaluate` then divides FI99 by the factor and multiplies by 1.6. The cycled run is therefore peak = 1.6 W + 1.6 C. The explicit sweep at a = 1.6 gives 0.3837 = 0.30 / 0.7818 (checked). The "seen through 1.6 / 2.0" reading (k 0.8) does not match the code. Within the doc's own convention, k 0.8 overstates the swing's effect by 2x. That error is on the safe side.
- **The explicit cyclic run (one set at a time, cinch scaled on its own)** is the table below. 'Doc' is the BL-039 chord at k 0.8. The line under the table is the doc's convention with the cinch explicit (preload mean ignored, swing range on top of 1.6 W). 'Goodman' is the frame's real cycle: walking 0 -> 1.6 g, cinch mean = preload, swing in phase, mapped to the R 0.1 coupon by linear Goodman on the knocked strength. ### Q2. The linear chord

- On this body the chord is above the explicit curve at every point of the sweep (largest excess 0.0120 at full cycling). Within its convention it is conservative. That is a *result*, not true "by construction": FI99 is a 99th percentile (`np.percentile(..., 99)`), not a max, and a percentile of convex functions is not convex. Re-check it whenever the body changes.
- It is **non-conservative** in three places. (1) Both of its endpoints leave out the cinch preload's mean stress and the R 0 / R 0.1 gap, so the doc's table is below the Goodman flip for every swing up to about 35 % (0 %: 0.2553 against 0.2926; 10 %: 0.2731 against 0.2986). (2) The cap at q = 1 is inconsistent with the doc's own k 0.8 mapping for swings above 72 %, where that mapping says the cinch passes the cycled run. (3) The table reads only the relative swing. Every value in it assumes a mean cinch of <= 150 N per anchor, so MT-1c must record the mean too. If the mean is higher, re-run with C scaled by mean / 150.

A coupon pulled at 22.3 deg at 1178 N proves only 1.109 at that angle, not 1.15. **Correction: lead the pull at 32 deg aft at 1178 N** (or keep 22 deg and raise the load to 1222 N).

At R 0.1 the same Goodman step needs about 1272 N at 32 deg. CA-1 itself stays at R 0.1, because it measures the material ratio as the record defines it.

### Q4. The release rule and the swing table

- **0.276 (0.2556 + 0.02) is not countersigned.** On the Goodman basis the R 0 / R 0.1 gap alone moves the body flip from 0.2553 to 0.2758 (x 1.081). The 150 N preload's mean stress moves it on to **0.2926** (signed lower bound 0.2654). At 0.276 the body margin is 0.943 (1.040 on the lower bound). That is below HA-LS-1's 1.0, or above it with less than the 0.02 cushion. Cheek and joint on the same basis: IM3 cheek 28 mm, design angle 0.2665; IM3 cheek 28 mm, DIR_SCATTER +10 deg 0.2762; z 234 joint (bolt boss, worst basis) 0.2606. The body governs. With a static cinch the flip + 0.02 rule gives **0.313**. The 1.15 scatter goal would need 0.337.
- **The swing table is replaced by the 'R1 CA-1 level' column above.** In practice: **R1 = CA-1 passes 0.40** (3/3 run-outs), and MT-1c shows a swing of <= 117 % with a mean of <= 150 N per anchor. Body margin at 0.40: 1.367 with the cinch static, 1.095 at a 100 % swing. The 0.40 bars are already in CA-1 step 3, so no new test is needed; R1 only waits for that run (3 x 27.8 h). If 0.40 fails, run 3 XY bars at the table level for the measured swing (>= 0.313). 0.276 on its own releases nothing (it covers 0 % swing). The old rows '<= 0.4 % -> 0.276', '<= 70 % -> 0.40' and '> 70 % -> 0.404' are withdrawn. Above a 117 % swing the level goes above 0.40 (0.429 at 150 %), and the cinch needs a re-run or a firmer strap.
