# What goes in a hunting bag: loadouts, access and pain points (Nov 2026)

Owner: pack-designer. Date 2026-11-08. Purpose: research step for the CORRIE packing study
(`designs/pack_5200/analysis/loadout_study.md`, data `designs/pack_5200/loadouts.yaml`). The owner asked "How can this
improve? Research what goes into a bag." Benchmarks are for **performance and use only**: no layout, name or trade
dress is copied.

**How to read the citations.** Every claim has a URL. Pages I fetched and read are cited plainly. Where the claim comes
from a search-engine summary of a page that I could not open (Rokslide returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher), it is marked
**(secondary)**. Retailer spec listings are marked **(retailer)**. Forum posts are anecdotes, not rates. oz -> g at
28.35. Short keys in brackets (e.g. [IA23]) are the `sources` keys in `loadouts.yaml`.

## 1. Summary for the designer

1. **Trip weights.** A 7-day backpack elk pack runs about **35-45 lb of gear plus 10-14 lb of food, 45-55 lb total**
   (secondary: [ProHunt](https://prohunt.pages.dev/articles/backcountry-elk-hunt-pack-list/), Rokslide
   [thread](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/backcountry-hunt-pack-weight.308322/page-2)). Itemised lists: an
   archery elk list at **30.7 lb** for 4 days with 3 L of water [IA23](https://initialascent.com/blogs/hunting-gear/2023-elk-gearlist-4-days-4-days);
   a 14-day sheep list at **41.1 lb packed** (52.0 lb with worn gear and rifle)
   [GH_SHEEP](https://www.gohunt.com/browse/gear-lists-and-reviews/gear-breakdown-for-a-successful-sheep-hunt);
   a guide's day pack with water at **25 lb** [EXO_20D](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/in-my-pack/20-day-elk-hunt-gear-list).
   Our three canonical loadouts come out at 10.7 / 19.2 / 21.6 kg (23.6 / 42.3 / 47.6 lb) carried, inside these bands.
2. **Food is the biggest variable.** 1.7-2.0 lb/day (3600 kcal ~ 1.8 lb)
   [EXO_RIFLE](https://www.exomtngear.com/blogs/article/hunting-gear-list-rifle-elk-mule-deer-in-october); 2 lb/day
   [EXO_20D](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/in-my-pack/20-day-elk-hunt-gear-list); 6 lb for 4 days (1.5 lb/day) [IA23].
3. **Water: 2-6 L a day, and most hunters carry both a bladder and a bottle.** Exo's rifle hunter keeps "about 6 liters
   at any time" across a Nalgene and a 4 L bag [EXO_RIFLE]; [IA23] carries 3 L in a bladder plus a 32 oz Nalgene.
   Forum consensus (secondary): bladder for sipping on the move, Nalgene as backup or on the hipbelt
   ([Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/nalgene-bottle-or-hydration-bladder-or-both.259696/)).
4. **The spotter needs its own reachable, protected home.** Every Stone Glacier pack has a designated spotter pocket
   or sleeve, internal or external, reached by a dedicated zip ([Stone Glacier](https://www.stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/stone-glacier-packs-spotting-scopes)).
   Exo puts the spotter in a full-length side pocket and the tripod in the lower side pocket "secured at the top by one
   of the pack's upper compression straps" ([Exo glassing setup](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/quick-and-efficient-glassing-setup)).
   Hunters worry about damage when the pack is set down hard and use cases or neoprene (secondary:
   [Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/how-do-you-guys-carry-your-spotter-in-your-pack-in-a-case-or-in-a-pocket-or-the-pack-unprotected.310501/)).
5. **Kill kit in one sack, reachable without unpacking.** "Keeping all these items together means you can get right to
   work" ([MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/gear/gear-hunt/ask-meateater-what-do-you-carry-in-a-mountain-hunting-pack));
   Exo keeps it in the pack's rear hydration compartment [EXO_RIFLE].
6. **Pain points that recur:** small items slide to the bottom; the item you need now is at the bottom (secondary:
   [Kryptek](https://kryptek.com/blogs/kryptekblog/the-ultimate-guide-to-organizing-your-hunting-backpack),
   [Initial Ascent](https://initialascent.com/blogs/hunting-tips/staying-organized-in-the-backcountry-a-system-that-works-when-it-matters));
   hipbelt pockets too small for a phone (secondary: [Mystery Ranch Forager reviews](https://packhacker.com/travel-gear/mystery-ranch/forager-hip-mini));
   metal-on-metal noise from the stove and fuel ([KUIU](https://kuiu.com/blogs/hunting-tips/proper-hunting-pack-organization));
   wet shelter next to the dry sleep system (secondary: [Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/packing-a-wet-tent.408090/),
   [REI](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/backpacking-in-the-rain.html)); blood soaking into the pack
   ([RMEF](https://rmef.org/?p=417533)).

### 2.4 Water

- Carry: 3 L (6.6 lb) in a 3 L bladder (3.2 oz) and a 32 oz Nalgene (6.5 oz) [IA23]; ~6 L capacity in a Nalgene + 4 L
  bag with a 42 mm filter [EXO_RIFLE]; two 32 oz Nalgenes + UV pen on a Dall sheep hunt
  [SG_LYLE](https://stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/lyles-10-day-alaska-dall-sheep-hunt-pack-dump);
  2 L bladder (5.6 oz) on a sheep hunt [GH_SHEEP].
- Daily use: 2-3 L cool or sitting, 4-6 L hot / steep / high (secondary:
  [Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/how-much-water-to-you-consume-on-backcountry-hunts.238302/post-2316125),
  [ProHunt water](https://prohunt.pages.dev/articles/backcountry-water-hunting-guide/)).
- Bladder vs bottle (secondary, Rokslide
  [thread 1](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/nalgene-bottle-or-hydration-bladder-or-both.259696/),
  [thread 2](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/hydration-bladders.139426/)): hunters drink more from a bladder because
  they don't stop; a bladder "takes up a lot of room in the pack, even in its own pocket"; a loose hose soaked one
  hunter's gear; a Nalgene is trusted more in a fall and is scoopable; "a Nalgene on the hip belt" with the bladder as
  storage is a common hybrid.
- Exo sells a hipbelt Nalgene holder and a hipbelt pouch for the rangefinder and snacks [EXO_RIFLE].
- Treatment: SteriPen 2.5 oz [IA23]; Sawyer Squeeze + dirty bags [EXO_20D].

### 2.5 Food

1.7-2.0 lb/day [EXO_RIFLE]; 2 lb/day [EXO_20D]; 1.5 lb/day [IA23]; ~2400 kcal/day on a Dall hunt [SG_LYLE]. Pack it
by day in a roll-top or zippered dry bag ([KUIU](https://kuiu.com/blogs/hunting-tips/proper-hunting-pack-organization));
"food and water close to the frame" (same source). Volume per day was not published: ASSUMPTION 0.5 kg/L.

## 3. Where optics ride

### 3.1 Binoculars and rangefinder: the chest

Every list puts the binos on a chest harness, often with a rangefinder pocket ([IA23], [GH_SHEEP], [SG_LYLE]:
"Skyline Bino Harness and Rangefinder Pocket"). The harness is not ours; the pack must not fight it (sternum strap
height, harness side straps).

### 3.2 Spotter and tripod

- Stone Glacier (maker's page): Solo 3600 "built-in ... spotting scope pocket" via the horseshoe zip; Col 4800 internal
  sleeve; Sky 5900 front pocket on a 22 in vertical zip; Sky Talus 6900 internal scope pockets behind #10 side zips;
  Kiowa 3200 internal sleeve that fits "any high-end spotter"; Approach 2800 exterior pocket up to a 115 mm objective
  ([Stone Glacier](https://www.stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/stone-glacier-packs-spotting-scopes)).
- Exo: spotter in the full-length side pocket; tripod in the lower side stretch pocket under an upper compression
  strap, "quick and quiet" ([Exo glassing](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/quick-and-efficient-glassing-setup),
  [Exo K4](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/why-do-k4-bags-have-minimal-internal-organization)).
- KUIU: inside the bag they "ride better ... but will not be quickly accessible"; use an external pocket for the scope
  and the opposite side pocket for the tripod ([KUIU](https://kuiu.com/blogs/hunting-tips/proper-hunting-pack-organization)).
- Wing / compression-panel pockets that take a scope: Eberlestock X2 "wing pockets" (secondary,
  [Ron Spomer](https://www.ronspomeroutdoors.com/blog/eberlestock-x2-pack-hunt-perfect)).
- Forum (secondary, [Rokslide spotter thread](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/how-do-you-guys-carry-your-spotter-in-your-pack-in-a-case-or-in-a-pocket-or-the-pack-unprotected.310501/)):
  under the lid for quick moves between glassing points, in a neoprene case in the bag for long hikes, "in the main
  bag sort of wrapped up"; an angled 88 mm Kowa did not fit one maker's side pockets; recurring worry about damage
  when the pack is set down on rock.

## 4. Pack-organization pain points

### 4.1 Things at the bottom, things you can't reach

"The thing you need now is at the bottom of the bag"; small items "slip through the gaps and slide toward the bottom"
(secondary: [Kryptek](https://kryptek.com/blogs/kryptekblog/the-ultimate-guide-to-organizing-your-hunting-backpack),
[Initial Ascent](https://initialascent.com/blogs/hunting-tips/staying-organized-in-the-backcountry-a-system-that-works-when-it-matters)).
Exo's answer is external pockets and an open main bag, with zip access for the layer and stove
([Exo K4](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/why-do-k4-bags-have-minimal-internal-organization)). Pockets you
cannot reach with the pack on are the recurring complaint already logged for CORRIE's pouch ([BA §6] in
`bag_body_architectures.md`). Hip-belt and strap pockets are the common fix (secondary:
[SectionHiker](https://sectionhiker.com/sectionhiker-gear-guide/10-best-backpack-accessory-pockets/)).

### 4.2 Hipbelt and phone pockets

Rangefinder pocket "a bit too small"; small pouch "a little too tight to easily fit my iPhone 13"; larger phones "too
big for" a secondary pocket (secondary: [Packhacker](https://packhacker.com/travel-gear/mystery-ranch/forager-hip-mini),
[OpticsPlanet reviews](https://www.opticsplanet.com/reviews/reviews-eberlestock-scout-bino-pack.html)). Exo's hipbelt
pouch keeps a phone or rangefinder within reach (secondary:
[Packstack](https://www.packstack.io/reviews/pack/exo-mountain-gear-k4-hipbelt-pouch)). Strap phone pockets are sold
separately (secondary: [Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/marsupial-backpack-strap-cell-phone-pocket.269011/)).

### 4.3 Noise and rattle

Stove, fuel and utensils together make noise; separate with soft items
([KUIU](https://kuiu.com/blogs/hunting-tips/proper-hunting-pack-organization)). Gear "bouncing around in your pack"
is never quiet (secondary: [Petersen's](https://www.petersenshunting.com/editorial/how-to-pack-smart-for-backpack-hunting/272233)).
Spotters are wrapped or cased against knocks (§3.2).

### 4.4 Wet gear

Wet shelter in its own contractor bag, outside the pack liner, away from the sleep system (secondary:
[Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/packing-a-wet-tent.408090/)); fly and inner in separate sacks
([REI](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/backpacking-in-the-rain.html)); strap it outside in mild weather
(secondary, same Rokslide thread).

### 4.5 Blood

Game bags keep meat out of the dirt; plastic "okay if it's cold out" but slows cooling; clean the pack soon, "Don't let
it sit in your elk's juices", dunk it in a creek ([RMEF](https://rmef.org/?p=417533)). A compactor bag around the
game bag kept packs clean for some; it can tear under heavy loads (secondary:
[Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/keeping-pack-clean-while-packing-out-meat.161055/page-2)).

## 6. Packing rules the leaders state

- Light, bulky, unbreakable low; heavier "above the hip belt"; food and water close to the frame; balance side to side
  ([KUIU](https://kuiu.com/blogs/hunting-tips/proper-hunting-pack-organization)).
- "Heavy items at the upper portion of the backpack and along your back"; fill air pockets with small items
  ([goHUNT](https://www.gohunt.com/browse/tips-and-tricks/backpacking/how-to-pack-and-organize-a-hunting-backpack)).
