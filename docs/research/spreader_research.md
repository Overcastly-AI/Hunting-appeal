# Load spreaders, framesheets and back panels: research for spreader v2 (BP-06)

Owner: structural-fea-engineer. Date: 2026-11-01. Feeds: `designs/pack_5200/analysis/spreader.md`.

**Method and honesty notes.** Brand pages first, then retailers, reviews and forum threads. Several
Rokslide pages and one retailer page did not return content to the fetch tool. Where a claim comes from a
search-engine summary of a page and not from the page itself, it is marked *(reported)*. Brands rarely
publish framesheet thickness or mass. Where nothing was found, this doc says so and does not fill the gap
from memory.

What this says:

1. **Two families.** Either the frame itself is a sheet (Kifaru ARK, KUIU), or there are stays plus a thin
   sheet or cross members (SG, Kifaru Duplex, Mystery Ranch, Seek Outside). Exo's K4 is reported to have
   no sheet at all. Nobody publishes the sheet mass.
2. **Thin sheets.** The thicknesses that are published are 1/16 in (Kifaru Duplex Lite) and 0.060 in HDPE
   (older Mystery Ranch). Carbon sheets are used where the sheet is the frame. Our 2.0 mm QI carbon sheet is
   thicker than anything published for a "spreader" role.
3. **Cross members, not area, keep bone ends out.** Seek Outside sells the cross stays specifically to
   reduce back intrusion. Our branch frame already has cross members (inboard branches, the z 330 bar). The
   spreader should **use them as supports**, not ignore them.
4. **Removability is the norm** for lumbar pads and cross stays (Seek Outside, Kifaru).
5. **DIY framesheet experience:** 1/32 in HDPE was "way too flimsy", 1/16 in "too much / too heavy"
   ([Backpacking Light](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/frame-sheet-hdpe-vs-abs-vs-umhw-vs/)).
   On inside cut-outs "drill out the corners so they are a radius and not a sharp inside corner cut.
   Otherwise this is a break point for further cracks" (same search, Dana framesheet thread
   [Backpacking Light](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/70357/)). → rounded corners and notch roots.

## 2. Complaints and pain points

- **Lumbar hot spots.** A Rokslide thread is titled "Kifaru Lumbar Irritation"
  ([Rokslide *(reported)*](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/kifaru-lumbar-irritation.221288/page-2)). Riders
  ask whether a small frame sheet in the lumbar pouch would "spread load from the stays"
  ([Rokslide Bikini frame *(reported)*](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/kifaru-bikini-frame.14210)).
  This is the point-load-from-a-stay problem: a stay printing through thin padding.
- **Frame contact.** A Seek Outside owner reported the frame "hit my butt" and the pack leaned back
  ([Rokslide *(reported)*](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/seekoutside-revolution.69493/)).
- **Bone ends.** No source was found that documents bone poke-through by brand. Guides advise bagging or
  boning meat, keeping heavy items high and against the back, and using a shelf or a strong lashing system
  ([Eastmans](https://blog.eastmans.com/how-to-haul-meat-on-a-pack-frame/),
  [Rokslide meat tips *(reported)*](https://www.rokslide.com/forums/threads/packing-out-de-boned-meat-video-and-tech-tip-thread.9225)).
  The bone-end case in HA-LS-1 remains an ASSUMPTION (20 % of 68 kg on a 22 mm patch).
- **Sweat and blood.** No brand data found. Engineering consequence used here: a **solid** sheet keeps meat
  juices and wet game bags on the bag side; cut-outs drain them onto the back gap. This counts against
  perforated options.

## 3. Skin-pressure thresholds for load carriage

| Threshold | Value | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Prolonged carriage limit used by this project | **14 kPa** | Golriz et al., Work 58 (2017), doi:10.3233/WOR-172651 (already cited in `back_comfort.md`) |
| Discomfort in 90 % of soldiers (shoulder, average) | 20 kPa | Bryant et al. (2001), cited by [Wettenschwiler et al., PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4631336/) |
| Blood occlusion | 16 kPa | Holloway et al. (1976), cited by Wettenschwiler 2015 |
| Ischemia, lower bound | 5.6–9.5 kPa | Sangeorzan et al. (1989), cited by Wettenschwiler 2015 |
| Peak static pressure predicts discomfort (≥ 85 % of variance) | — | Wettenschwiler 2015 |
| Measured peaks, 25 kg pack | ~90 kPa | as summarised in the same search |

The project keeps **14 kPa peak** at the 1.6 g walking load. 16 kPa (occlusion) sits just above it.

Soft-tissue stiffness for the skin model (no back-specific value found): effective indentation moduli of
**11–13 kPa** at the abdomen ([PolyU hand-held indentation](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/a-hand-held-indentation-system-for-the-assessment-of-mechanical-p/))
and **10.4–89.2 kPa** on the lower limb, rising with muscle and in men
([Effective elastic properties for lower limb soft tissues](https://www.scilit.net/publications/28f0aabc780ba8ce95797fdac2073c58)).
The spreader study uses **60 kPa** (stiff end: less spreading, higher peak) with **20 kPa** as the soft
sensitivity. ASSUMPTION: 25 mm tissue column.

## 6. Patents

No patent search result was specific to a bag-side load spreader on a split-stay frame. The hunting-pack
apparatus result returned ([ipiq 3693496](https://ipiq.io/patents/3693496)) is a generic hunting backpack
apparatus. A sheet spanning stays is long-standing prior art (framesheets in Dana Design, Mystery Ranch and
Kifaru packs above). The v2 part uses a flat sheet, edge tubes and clips; none of it copies a brand's shape.

## 7. Takeaways for BP-06

1. Use the frame's own cross members (branches, bar) as supports. Brands with cross stays credit them with
   keeping the back clear.
2. Thin is normal (1.6 mm class). A sandwich gets the stiffness at a fraction of the mass, if bone-end core
   crush and core shear are checked.
3. Round every corner and notch root (DIY framesheet experience: square inside corners crack).
4. Keep it solid: a blood and sweat barrier, and no bone path.
5. Make it removable, toolless.
6. Stays print through thin foam. Riders ask for a sheet to "spread load from the stays". The skin-pressure
   check must model the stay on the foam, not a uniform pad pressure.
