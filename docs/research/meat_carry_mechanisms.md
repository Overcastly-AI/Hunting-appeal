# Meat carry between bag and frame: how the leaders do it (and do they swing?)

Research for `designs/pack_5200/analysis/meat_carry_alternatives.md`. Date: 2026-11-06. Owner trigger: the
3D render of our "between mode" (bag pivots about 28.5 deg on the z 60 hinge pins) - "I do not like the swing
motion. Research other bags."

This file **extends** `docs/research/bag_attachment_research.md` (2026-11-05), which covers the bag-to-frame
hardware, top anchoring, quiet and the patent table. It does not repeat that. Here the question is narrower: **when
meat goes between the bag and the frame, which way does the bag move, what holds it there, what does the user do,
and what do owners complain about.**

## How far to trust this

- Brand pages and the patents' own text first, then reviews, then forums. Every claim has a URL.
- **Rokslide returned 403 to the fetch tool again** (as on 2026-11-05). Rokslide claims come from search-engine
  summaries of the thread and are marked ***(reported)***. Treat each one as one owner's account.
- Brand how-to **videos** (Stone Glacier "How to: Use the Load Shelf", Exo "How To: Access the Load Shelf",
  Seek Outside assembly video) could not be watched. Where the brand text is silent about the direction of motion,
  this file says **"not stated"** and does not fill it in.
- Patent status and expiry are Google Patents' estimates (Google says so). Engineering read, not a legal opinion.

Units: mm, kg, N internally, imperial alongside.

## 1. Pack by pack

### 1.1 Stone Glacier (Krux / Xcurve / R3 frames: Load Shelf; Terminus: Load Cell)

**Mechanism.**
- Every SG frame has a Load Shelf that lets you "extend the bag from the frame"; it is a shelf between bag and
  frame, and it can take "as big as the hindquarters of a moose"
  ([SG Load Cell vs Load Shelf](https://stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/load-cell-vs-load-shelf),
  [SG Load Shelf page](https://stoneglacier.com/pages/load-shelf)).
- The bag is held to the frame by "12 connection points to the frame via 1" webbing" and Duraflex buckles
  ([SG Load Shelf page](https://stoneglacier.com/pages/load-shelf)). The same straps are lengthened to open the gap;
  extending them adds "more than 2,500 cubic inches" (41 L)
  ([SG One Frame](https://www.stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/one-frame-for-every-hunt), cited 2026-11-05).
- **Direction of motion: not stated by SG.** The steps below (separate, lay flat, load, re-attach bottom first)
  describe a bag that comes **off the frame face and goes back on over the meat**, i.e. it translates aft on long
  straps. No hinge is named. One summary of SG guidance says to load "with the pack lying flat and the bag rolled back"
  ([search summary of SG instructions](https://stoneglacier.com/pages/instructions) ***(reported)***).
- **Terminus (sewn bag):** no gap at all. A 2200 cu in (36 L) collapsible **Load Cell inside the main bag, next to
  the frame**, "positions the meat next to the frame and closest to your center of gravity", UHMWPE fabric, two drain
  holes, boned-out meat only, and it uses main-bag space
  ([SG Load Cell vs Load Shelf](https://stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/load-cell-vs-load-shelf),
  [SG Terminus 8700](https://stoneglacier.com/products/terminus-8700)).

**User steps (SG's own words).** "Start by separating the bag from the frame to unveil the Load Shelf." "Once the meat
is loaded into the shelf, start by tightening the Load Shelf itself." "Reattach the bag starting with the bottom straps
and work your way up to the top of the pack." Lifters last, once worn
([SG complete guide](https://stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/the-complete-guide-to-stone-glacier-hunting-packs)).
Load the shelf last, after the gear is in the bag, then compress around the frame
([search summary, SG guide / Rokslide](https://www.rokslide.com/?p=48) ***(reported)***).

**Physics claim (useful number).** SG's sandbag test: 76 lb (34.5 kg) loaded low and full-width gave "around 32
pounds of constant force (leverage)"; the same load stacked on the Load Shelf and cinched gave "just over 14 pounds",
i.e. "cut the amount of leverage in half"
([SG Physics of Pack Load](https://www.stoneglacier.com/blogs/the-vestibule/the-physics-of-pack-load)).
SG's placement rule: keep the meat "an even thickness the height of the frame, as close to your spine as possible"
(Kurt Racicot, SG, in [search summary](https://www.rokslide.com/?p=48) ***(reported)***).

**Pain points.** Time to separate and re-attach the bag is "the main negative"; another owner disliked "the extra
time, the bulging load, and the shift in center of gravity"
([Rokslide "Why Load Shelf?"](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/why-load-shelf.383056/) ***(reported)***).
Threading buckles to re-attach ([HuntingBC](https://www.huntingbc.ca/forum/archive/index.php/t-93182.html), 2026-11-05
file). Terminus cell hard to clean (2026-11-05 file).

### 1.2 Mystery Ranch (OVERLOAD: Metcalf, Beartooth, Pop Up; Guide Light MT / NICE frames)

**Mechanism.** OVERLOAD is the "separation of bag and frame, the bulk of the weight in between": "Build a flexible
shelf, elongate the straps" ([MR Lightening the Load](https://www.mysteryranch.com/post/lightening-the-load)).
The shelf is an "expanded shelf (made of 330-denier CORDURA just like the pack)"
([GearJunkie Terraframe](https://gearjunkie.com/packs/mystery-ranch-terraframe-50-review)).
The patent description gives the geometry: a **sling permanently attached to the bag's front lower edge**, its
distal straps buckle to a frame cross member; the bag-to-frame spacing D is "generally variable between about zero
inches and about 12 inches or more" (0-305 mm), set by the sling length plus **load straps and compression straps
that also position the bag top**. The patent describes only D changing; it does **not** describe a pivot
([US 8,579,171](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8579171); same description in
[US 10,952,523](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10952523)). So the bag **moves aft as a whole, on straps, with the
sling as its floor**; the top is held by load lifters / load straps cinched over the cargo.

**User steps.** Owners: unclip the side clips and "a small clip directly behind the bag", the bag comes away
([search summary of Metcalf reviews](https://www.activegearreview.com/camping-gear/backpack/mystery-ranch-metcalf-review/) ***(reported)***).
On the old Guide Light frame the load lifters had to be undone to separate bag and frame; the MT frame removed that
step ([Black Ovis](https://new.blackovis.com/community/brand-highlights/new-mystery-ranch-guide-light-mt-frame/), 2026-11-05 file ***(reported)***).
Generic steps for this class: "Unclip the side compression straps, loosen the lower compression straps, and disconnect
the load lifter straps", lift the bag off, load, re-attach
([goHUNT meat shelf](https://gohunt.com/browse/gear-lists-and-reviews/load-it-up-extending-your-hunt-in-the-backcountry-and-utilizing-your-meat-shelf)).

**Pain points.**
- **Rides low.** "The integrated load shelf sits too low on the frame to keep a small sand bag from dropping below
  the hip belt"; "it attaches near the bottom of the frame rather than a quarter or third of the way up"
  ([Backwoods Pursuit women's Metcalf](https://backwoodspursuit.com/womens-mystery-ranch-metcalf-review/)).
- **Access.** The load-securing straps "wrap over the top of the pack", which blocks the lid and main bag in OVERLOAD
  ([GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/packs/mystery-ranch-terraframe-50-review)).
- MR's own fit advice: carry the frame high so the lifters run near 45 deg on heavy hauls; and do not just
  "sandwich" meat behind a bag full of personal gear
  ([search summary of MR / hunting articles](https://www.mysteryranch.com/blog/post/lightening-the-load) ***(reported)***).

### 1.3 Kifaru (Duplex / ARK frames; Grab-It; bags with optional meat shelf)

**Mechanism.** The bag is held by the same compression straps that hold the load (Guide Lid kit: "Four 30" compression
straps with Polymer K-Clips"; ARK straps girth-hitch through the frame holes)
([Kifaru kits](https://kifaru.net/products/guide-lid-and-compression-kits)). Owners: any Kifaru bag can "sandwich the
meat in between the bag and frame"; compression straps hold it, or "the Grab It to hold the bottom and the lash kit
to hold the bag against the meat"
([Rokslide Kifaru Meat Hauling](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/kifaru-meat-hauling.123533/post-1164501) ***(reported)***).
A Grab-It used as a shelf "will move the bottom connection of the bag about 4" [100 mm] away from the frame"; one
owner preferred it to a shelf because it "kept the meat tighter to the frame and since the weight from the meat isn't
passed on to the bag there is no sagging"
([Rokslide Grab-It threads](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/kifaru-22-mag-w-a-grab-it-load-shelf.72435/post-748187) ***(reported)***).
**Direction:** the bag is lifted off and laid back over the meat; no hinge.

**Pain points.** "A ton of straps"; strap and frame-loop squeaks; meat shelves "a gimmicky element" to some owners
(2026-11-05 file; [Rokslide](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/why-do-kifaru-owners-hate-load-shelves.148457/) ***(reported)***).

### 1.4 Exo Mountain Gear (K4)

**Mechanism.** Four pairs of frame straps, alternating "bag connection, frame compression, bag connection, frame
compression"; orange-bartacked frame straps hold the **meat to the frame on their own**
([Exo quick tip](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/k4-quick-tip-frame-compression-straps)).
Exo's shelf access: "Disconnect the side-release buckles", "Loosen or remove the lid", "Lift the bag over the frame",
"Secure your load between the bag and frame"
([Exo shelf access](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/k4-load-shelf-access)). A reviewer: release the lid "held by
the top hooks", unbuckle the sides, tighten the cargo to the frame with "dedicated straps", reattach the bag; load
"wedged in a V-shaped space between frame and bag"; drop the bottom compression strap to make a shelf
([Carryology K4](https://www.carryology.com/reviews-2/exo-mtn-gear-k4-review/)).
**Direction:** both. Exo's own text lifts the bag **over** the frame (translation). An owner's field method "leaves the
bottom straps attached, lets the bag open like a hinge", sets meat on the frame, cinches it with "the two heavy-duty
compression straps", then reattaches the bag to compress it further
([Western Hunter K4 7200](https://westernhunter.net/gear-reviews/exo-mtn-gear-k4-7200-pack-system/)).
**So the hinge/V is a common field habit on strap packs, but it is a loose-strap hinge, not a pinned one, and the
meat is strapped to the frame before the bag closes.** "The conversion takes only a few seconds"
([Outside Bozeman](https://outsidebozeman.com/gear/product-reviews/misc/review-exo-k4-5000-hunting-pack)).

**Pain points.** Top re-attach over meat is where K4s get broken (dealer ~18 cases, 2026-11-05 file ***(reported)***);
straps "super tight" to reach over a full shelf (same file); buckled "the wrong buckles together by accident"
(colour-coding asked for); only two meat straps make loose meat or a head "trickier"
([Western Hunter](https://westernhunter.net/gear-reviews/exo-mtn-gear-k4-7200-pack-system/)).

### 1.5 Seek Outside (Revolution frame, load sling / load panel); Paradox Packs (Evolution)

**Mechanism.** The Revolution ships with "a load sling", "an over the top strap" and six compression straps
([SO Revolution](https://seekoutside.com/revolution-suspension-and-frame/)). A load panel clips with Gatekeepers to
any of several loops on the frame panel, which sets the **load height**
([search summary, BPL / SO](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/see-outside-unaweep-or-revolution/) ***(reported)***).
The bag's two top straps run through tri-glides on the frame to a buckle behind the head, and "It also requires the
use of the top straps to keep a moderate to large load from flopping around"
([Bedrock & Paradox Revolution review](https://bedrockandparadox.com/2017/11/30/seek-outside-revolution-review/)).
**Direction:** the bag rides on the compression straps over the load; no hinge stated.

**Paradox Evolution.** A load shelf "attached to the bottom of the back panel" clips to the Talon (compression
panel); owners carried meat "load shelf, packbag, talon", most on the Talon; one asked whether the bag and Talon were
"completely detached from the frame and relying only on the compression straps"
([search summary, Rokslide Paradox threads](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/paradox-packs.11570/page-3) ***(reported)***).
Rationale quoted there: "I can compress the meat against the frame by itself instead of compressing all of my gear
against the meat and then against the frame."

**Pain points.** "If meat isn't held rigorously in place it tends to slide down, ball up towards the bottom"; the
Evolution mesh "bulged ... and pressed out against the wearer"; a structural pocket held "only by a seam"
([Bedrock & Paradox](https://bedrockandparadox.com/2017/11/30/seek-outside-revolution-review/)).
The over-the-top Y strap "is NOT long enough to easily pass over a fully loaded engorged pack"; Gatekeepers "aren't
really designed to attach two loops meeting each other" and "limited compression"
([BPL SO thread p3](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/see-outside-unaweep-or-revolution/page/3/)).

### 1.6 KUIU (ICON 6 / ICON PRO load sling mode)

**Mechanism and steps (KUIU's own list, ICON 6).** 1 "Detach and extend compression straps (2 top, 4 side)." 2 Peel
back the lumbar pad. 3 "Detach bag attachment straps (2) from waist belt" and stow them on the bag's hook-and-loop
patches. 4 "Remove upper frame stays and compression straps from frame-stay pockets." 5 "Lay the frame flat and place
your load." 6 "Bypass the frame stay pockets and connect the top straps. Connect side compression straps. Cinch tight."
Tip: "first tighten the lower compression straps to create a V-shaped compression zone"; the result is "a full-frame
compression sleeve"
([KUIU ICON 6 article](https://kuiu.com/blogs/innovation/the-latest-advancements-in-hunting-pack-design),
[KUIU load sling mode](https://www.kuiu.com/blogs/innovation/kuiu-pro-pack-system-load-sling-mode)).
**Direction:** the bag is **fully released from the frame and belt, the load goes on the flat frame, and the bag is
laid back over it**, held by 2 top + 4 side straps. It floats; nothing pivots. PRO adds +2500 cu in (41 L)
(2026-11-05 file).

**Pain points.** Quarters hard to keep above the belt, the bag "seems to sag a little"; owners add their own frame
straps; buckles broken cinching heavy loads; "bag attaches to frame weird" (2026-11-05 file ***(reported)***). KUIU's
2012 rep note: bulky bone-in quarters are too thick for the sling and go in the bag (2026-11-05 file ***(reported)***).

### 1.7 Eberlestock (Brute "Open-Away", Team Elk load panel, Mainframe; patent '885)

**Mechanism.** Brute: "unclip the main bag from the frame. The bag slides away, creating a dedicated space next to the
frame" ([Atomic Defense Brute](https://www.atomicdefense.com/collections/eberlestock-brute-hunting-packs) ***(retailer copy)***).
Team Elk: "a sewn-in load panel allows the pack to pull away from the frame"
([Locally Team Elk](https://www.locally.com/product/1433360/eberlestock-team-elk-mountain-pack) ***(retailer copy)***).
The patent (§2) adds the mechanics: a fabric panel that folds **accordion-style** between frame and bag when retracted,
extended by lengthening two bottom straps (run parallel from frame to bag **under** the panel, through loops sewn on its
underside) and two pairs of side straps, plus releasing cap straps; frame and bag can be laid flat in a line
([US 12,495,885](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12495885)). **Direction: the bag translates aft on parallel straps
over a fabric floor (the claimed "load sling").**

**Pain points.** "the strapping system isn't very good for multiple antlers"; a broken buckle (2026-11-05 file). A
scabbard slid between bag and frame "can make the whole system feel unstable when loaded"
([search summary, HuntTalk](https://www.hunttalk.com/threads/a-solo-hunt-for-the-gals.279187/post-2656925) ***(reported)***).

### 1.8 Initial Ascent

**Mechanism.** Bag off; meat in the **Pannier Load Carrier** on the bare frame; "Lash the pannier down tightly";
"strap the bag with gear over the top", keeping gear clean of blood
([Born Hunting](https://bornhunting.com/tested-initial-ascent-backpacks/)). The Pannier "allows the user to carry the
meat between the frame and the bag"; Rokslide's (IA-sponsored) review ran Wyoming elk loads "up to 120lbs" (54 kg)
([search summary, Rokslide IA review](https://www.rokslide.com/initial-ascent-backpack-review/) ***(reported)***).
Pannier $63.99, standard only with the IA Day Pack
([IA Pannier](https://initialascent.com/products/the-pannier-load-carrier) ***(reported)***).
**Direction:** bag off, then laid over the meat. No hinge.

**Pain points.** Extra part to buy; a broken cinch cord on one unit; lid strap came off; lost frame strap (2026-11-05
file, ***(reported)***).

### 1.9 Argali

No Argali framed pack found (2026-11-05 file). Argali sells game bags, not frames. Not covered.

## 3. Synthesis

### 3.1 Which way the bag moves

| pack | how the gap opens | what holds the meat | what holds the bag | bag pivots on a pin? | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stone Glacier | bag separated, re-attached bottom-first on lengthened straps | Load Shelf straps around the frame | 12 webbing points, lengthened | no (direction not stated) | SG guide |
| Mystery Ranch OVERLOAD | bag spaced aft 0-305 mm on a sling + load straps | fabric sling (shelf) at the bag's lower front + compression | sling, load straps, lifters | no | US 8,579,171 |
| Kifaru | bag lifted off, laid over meat | compression straps / Grab-It / lash kit | same straps | no | Rokslide (rep.) |
| Exo K4 | bag lifted over the frame (brand) or opened like a loose-strap hinge (owner) | frame-only straps (orange bartack) | bag-connection strap pairs + lid on top hooks | no pin; loose hinge is an owner habit | Exo, Western Hunter |
| Seek Outside | bag over the load on compression straps | load sling / panel at chosen loops | 6 compression + over-the-top strap | no | SO, B&P |
| KUIU ICON | bag fully released, frame laid flat, bag laid back over | the bag + straps ("V-shaped compression zone") | 2 top + 4 side straps | no | KUIU |
| Eberlestock | bag slides away on parallel straps | fabric load sling (patented) | 4 side + 2 bottom straps | no | US 12,495,885 |
| Initial Ascent | bag off, pannier on frame, bag strapped over | Pannier lashed to frame | straps over the top | no | Born Hunting |
| **Ours today** | **bag pivots 28.5 deg on the z 60 pins** | spreader + IM1-IM3 + cammed under-straps | pins + W1-W3 | **yes** | `meat_haul_modes.md` A2 |

**Finding 1. No leader pins the bag on a bottom hinge for the meat mode.** Everyone either (a) translates the bag aft
on lengthened straps over a floor (SG, MR, Eberlestock) or (b) takes the bag off, fixes the meat to the frame, and lays
the bag back over it on compression straps (KUIU, IA, Kifaru, Exo's own steps, SO). The V or wedge shape exists
(Exo review, KUIU tip) but it comes from cinching the lower straps first, not from a pivot.

**Finding 2. The best-liked haul behaviour is "meat to the frame first, bag second".** Exo's frame-only straps, the
Kifaru Grab-It owner ("weight from the meat isn't passed on to the bag ... no sagging"), the Paradox owner ("compress
the meat against the frame by itself"), IA's pannier, KUIU owners adding frame straps. Our IM1-IM3 eye-to-eye straps
and the pin-to-pin cradle already do this. The bag then only has to be held, not to carry the meat.

**Finding 3. The recurring pain points are the same five.**
1. **Sag / rides low** (MR shelf "below the hip belt", KUIU "sag", B&P "ball up towards the bottom").
2. **Flop / top not held** (SO "flopping around" without top straps; K4 top re-attach breaks).
3. **Straps too short to reach over a full load** (K4 "super tight", SO Y-strap "NOT long enough").
4. **Time and fiddling** (SG "time to separate and reattach", MR "messing around", Kifaru "a ton of straps").
5. **Wrong buckle / colour confusion** (Exo).

**Finding 4. Leverage numbers.** SG measured leverage halving (32 to 14 lb) when the same load was stacked high and
cinched on the shelf instead of low and wide. MR / goHUNT / SG all say keep meat high and an even thickness the height
of the frame. The one sourced complaint about shelf height is that it attaches "near the bottom of the frame".

### 3.2 What this means for our swing

- The swing itself is our invention of necessity (the bag is pinned at z 60, so the easiest way to open a gap is to
  rotate). The field does not need it. Its costs, read against the findings: the gap is a wedge (71 mm at IM1 to
  250 mm at IM3), so the meat's thick end is forced high and its narrow end low whatever the meat's shape; the bag's
  upper aft corner travels furthest from the back (the largest lever); the hood cannot re-engage (the K4 lesson
  forced "do not re-clip"); and the bag top is held only by W3.
- What the field gives instead: **parallel stand-off** (SG, MR, Eberlestock: bag held aft at a set distance at more
  than one height) or **float** (KUIU, IA: bag laid over meat already fixed to the frame). Our hard points (z 60 pins,
  IM3 eyes, W1-W3 haul lengths, the pin-to-pin cradle) can support both without a fabric shelf.

## 4. Gaps

- Brand videos not watched; SG and Exo direction of motion is inferred from their step text, as stated above.
- Rokslide still read through search summaries only.
- No leader publishes the bag-to-frame gap in mm or the time for the meat mode, except MR's patent (0-12+ in) and
  Exo's "a few seconds" (review). A timed gloved trial is still our differentiator.
- Patents: claims read on Google Patents. FTO read on US 12,495,885 and the US 8,579,171 lapse still open.
