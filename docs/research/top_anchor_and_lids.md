# Top anchor and lid: how framed hunting packs hold the bag top and carry the lid (BL-029)

pack-designer, 2026-11-12. Research for `designs/pack_5200/analysis/corrie_f_body.md` section 14 (CORRIE F rev 3).
It adds to `docs/research/bag_attachment_research.md` (2026-11-05, section 1), which is cited here rather than repeated.

## How this was researched, and how far to trust it

- **Order of trust.** Brand pages first, then reviews, then forum threads, then patents. Every claim has a URL.
- **Blocked pages.** Rokslide (every thread and review page) and sectionhiker.com returned **403** to the fetch tool on
  2026-11-12. Claims from those pages come from a search-engine summary and are marked ***(secondary)***. Treat each
  as one owner's account, not a brand statement. Initial Ascent's lid page returned 404. Its claim is secondary too.
- **Not found.** Exo publishes no drawing of how the K4 lid's straps are routed. No brand publishes its lid's strap
  angles. Anything not found is marked "not found". Nothing here comes from memory.
- **Patents.** Read on Google Patents. The legal status shown there is Google's estimate. This is not a
  freedom-to-operate opinion. A patent attorney reads the claims before tooling, as DECISIONS 2026-11-01 already
  requires.
- **Benchmark only.** Performance and function are benchmarked here, never the look. Section 7 describes a visual
  language in general terms. It does not describe any brand's trade dress, and none is to be copied.

Units: mm, g, L, with in, oz and cu in alongside (1 L = 61.0 cu in).

## 1. Exo Mountain Gear K4 (and K3)

**The lid as a part**
- K4 lid: "648 cubic inches of storage capacity" (10.6 L), **8.1 oz (230 g)**, 12 × 11 × 5 in (305 × 279 × 127 mm),
  two zipped pockets, 500D Cordura and YKK zips. It "functions as a compression panel" for a skull and antlers held
  between bag and lid ([Exo K4 Lid](https://exomtngear.com/lid)).
- It ships with every K4 system except the 2200 daypack, which needs a separate "K4 Lid Strap Set" to take a lid
  ([Exo K4 Lid](https://exomtngear.com/lid)).
- On the 3600 system the lid adds "almost 650 cu-in" on top of the bag. It "can be attached to all K4 bags, even the
  small one", using strap extenders on the smallest bag
  ([Carryology K4 review](https://www.carryology.com/reviews-2/exo-mtn-gear-k4-review/)).

**How the lid and bag top attach**
- The lid "stays perfectly in place when cinched down with front and back straps". To reach the shelf, you "release
  the lid held by the top hooks"
  ([Carryology K4 review](https://www.carryology.com/reviews-2/exo-mtn-gear-k4-review/)). Shelf access in Exo's own
  words, step 2: "Loosen or remove the lid"
  ([Exo shelf access](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/k4-load-shelf-access)).
- The frame has "4 pairs of integrated compression straps". Top to bottom the order is "bag connection, frame
  compression, bag connection, frame compression"
  ([Exo quick tip](https://exomtngear.com/blogs/article/k4-quick-tip-frame-compression-straps)).
- Without a lid, Exo's **Packout Straps** (1 × 30 in, 2.0 oz the pair, steel G-hook) run "from the top of the frame to
  the top of the bag" to hold a head and antlers
  ([Exo Packout Straps](https://exomtngear.com/products/k4-packout-straps)). So Exo's own fix for a lidless top is a
  strap from the frame top over the load to the bag top: **the lid's straps are the top anchor.**
- Exo staff said buckles "have more play than ladder lock connections", so ladder locks hold the lid snug to the frame
  top. Owners describe the **lid as the bridge** between frame top and bag top
  ([Rokslide K4 vs K3](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/k4-improvement-vs-k3.313383/latest) ***(secondary)***,
  quoted in `bag_attachment_research.md` 1.2).
- An Exo representative calls the K4 lid a **"floating lid"** that "fits well and stays put", with quick disconnects;
  a reviewer prefers it for strapping heads and antlers to the top
  ([Rokslide K4 thread p81](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/exo-mountain-k4.279017/page-81) ***(secondary)***).
- Exo's designer said that keeping K3-style buckles at the lid "wasn't an option" with the K4 design
  ([Rokslide K4 thread p82](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/exo-mountain-k4.279017/page-82) ***(secondary)***).

**Likes and dislikes**
- Likes: "a classic design with no frills and no gimmicks"; spacious
  ([Carryology](https://www.carryology.com/reviews-2/exo-mtn-gear-k4-review/)). It does not flop around; the K4 lid's
  extra volume over K3 comes from more height
  ([Rokslide K4 thread p30](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/exo-mountain-k4.279017/page-30) ***(secondary)***).
  Owners call the K4's lid attachment points "hugely improved" over earlier models
  ([Rokslide K4 vs K3](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/k4-improvement-vs-k3.313383/latest) ***(secondary)***).
- Dislikes: the lid is not weatherproof ("the lid or side pockets are still unprotected",
  [Carryology](https://www.carryology.com/reviews-2/exo-mtn-gear-k4-review/)). Re-attaching the bag top with meat in
  the shelf is where owners break things ([Rokslide K4 issue](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/exo-k4-pack-issue.410398/page-2)
  ***(secondary)***, `bag_attachment_research.md` 1.2). Some owners prefer the K3's bag-and-lid attachment
  ([Rokslide K4 thread p81](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/exo-mountain-k4.279017/page-81) ***(secondary)***).

## 2. Stone Glacier

- The **EVO lid** has side-release buckles on the front and two "tension locks" (ladder locks) on the back
  ([1 Shot Gear, EVO lid](https://1shotgear.com/products/stone-glacier-evo-series-lid)). The rear straps take the
  cinching; the front straps only clip.
- Stone Glacier sells a **compression strap extender kit** that can "replace the lid for top compression to drop
  weight": the tension lock attaches to the bag's rear lid strap and the side-release buckles clip to the top loops on
  the front of the bag ([Stone Glacier extender kit](https://stoneglacier.com/products/stone-glacier-compression-strap-extender-kit)).
  Again the lid's strap path, frame side to bag front, is the top compression whether or not a lid is on.
- The **Hydro Lid** detaches and carries as its own small pack on Stone Glacier shoulder straps
  ([goHUNT, Hydro Lid](https://shop.gohunt.com/products/stone-glacier-hydro-lid)).
- Owners: switching between lid mode and hauling on the shelf means unthreading straps, swapping them with the load
  lifters and re-threading them ([Rokslide backpack blasphemy](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/backpack-blasphemy.279236/post-2747929)
  ***(secondary)***). The top strap "wants to slip off the top" of the frame when it runs horizontally
  ([Rokslide X Kurve](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/stone-glacier-x-kurve.58677/page-2) ***(secondary)***,
  `bag_attachment_research.md` 1.1). Some prefer the lid to a side pocket because it is lighter and "keeps weight
  closer to your body" ([Rokslide Solo review](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/stone-glacier-solo-review.220964/)
  ***(secondary)***).

## 3. Kifaru

- The **Guide Lid** attaches "directly to any Duplex Frame" with a kit of four 30 in compression straps with K-clips;
  the extra webbing lets you "adjust the height of the Guide Lid along the Duplex Frame" and compress loose gear between
  lid and frame ([Kifaru kits](https://kifaru.net/products/guide-lid-and-compression-kits)). So Kifaru's lid hangs on
  the frame, not on the bag.
- The **Reckoning** bag needs no lid: lid tabs take buckles, and the vertical compression straps hook to them when the
  collar is folded over ([Rokslide Reckoning review](https://www.rokslide.com/kifaru-reckoning-review/) ***(secondary)***).
- The **Sherman pocket** can mount on the top; on one owner's Woodsman its top straps go to the sides of the frame. A
  worry is that compressing from a lid so high up is hard
  ([Rokslide, Kifaru lids](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/kifaru-lids.12100/page-2) ***(secondary)***).

## 4. Seek Outside

- **Backpack Top Lid:** 500 cu in (8.2 L) main chamber plus a removable flip-out zipped security pocket, **6 oz
  (170 g)** with pocket and straps. It is held by "two long Gatekeeper Straps" that secure the lid "to the bottom of
  your backpack" ([Seek Outside top lid](https://seekoutside.com/backpack-top-lid/)). The lid's straps run the full
  height of the bag, so the lid is a strap over the whole load.
- The Revolution kit includes an **over-the-top strap**, the heaviest strap in the kit, with three connections
  ([Seek Outside Revolution](https://seekoutside.com/revolution-suspension-and-frame/), and an SO employee on
  [Backpacking Light](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/see-outside-unaweep-or-revolution/page/3/)
  ***(secondary)***; `bag_attachment_research.md` 1.5).

## 5. Mystery Ranch

- Metcalf: removable lid with zipped pockets, and an "Overload" shelf between frame and bag
  ([Sportsman's Guide, Metcalf](https://www.sportsmansguide.com/product/index/mystery-ranch-metcalf-75-pack?a=3018996),
  [Black Ovis, Metcalf](https://www.blackovis.com/mystery-ranch-metcalf-hunting-pack)). Using the Overload with its load
  straps "does limit on-trail access to the lid and full pack body"
  ([GearJunkie Terraframe review](https://gearjunkie.com/packs/mystery-ranch-terraframe-50-review)).
- The Ultra Light Metcalf's lid fits only the newer frame because its roll top uses male and female buckles, which
  changes the lid's buckle layout ([Mystery Ranch UL daypack lid](https://www.MysteryRanch.com/ultra-light-daypack-lid-pack)).
  That is one data point that **the lid and the roll closure are designed together**.
- An owner found two spare buckles in a lid pocket and could not tell what they were for
  ([Rokslide Metcalf first impression](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/mystery-ranch-metcalf-just-arrived-first-impression.95353/post-928648)
  ***(secondary)***).

## 7. Look: what makes a lid read as part of the bag

This section is design analysis, not a sourced claim. It states principles. It does not describe any brand's look.

1. **Footprint follows the bag top.** The lid's plan outline is the bag's top outline (same width, same corner radius),
   not a smaller box placed on it. A lid narrower than the roll reads as a separate item; a lid flush with the side
   walls reads as the bag's cap. (For scale only: Exo's lid is 305 × 279 mm in plan,
   [Exo K4 Lid](https://exomtngear.com/lid); the bag widths it sits on were not found.)
2. **A crown, not a box.** The top surface is domed in both directions and the side wall tapers to nothing at the
   front and rear, so the lid has no vertical box wall on the frame side or at the corners. The roll under it is
   rounded, so the lid follows the roll.
3. **One line runs through.** The straps that hold the lid continue the bag's lines: they leave the frame, cross the
   lid in channels, and land on the bag's aft face at the same x, so there is one continuous strap line from frame to
   bag. A strap that stops at a buckle on the lid's edge breaks it.
4. **Panel lines match.** The lid's front edge sits on a seam of the bag (here the hinge band B-02h, above the door), so
   the lid's edge and the bag's panel break line up. The lid's side seam is at the same height on both sides.
5. **Colour blocking shared.** The lid uses the bag's two fabrics in the bag's proportions: solid body, camo only
   where the bag already uses camo (the wear-zone and blade accents, O-6). A camo band on the lid's front lip, in line
   with the D2 camo blades below it, ties lid and door together. A lid in a different colour or a full-camo lid on a
   solid bag reads as an add-on.
6. **Hardware hidden or repeated.** The lid shows no hardware of its own; the only visible hardware is the crown straps'
   ladder locks, which repeat the W1-W3 hardware.

These rules are what section 14 of `corrie_f_body.md` applies to rev 3.

## 8. What this means for CORRIE F

- Every peer that carries a lid holds it with straps that run from the **frame side** over the load to the **bag's
  front (aft) face**, and three of them (Exo Packout Straps, Stone Glacier extender kit, Seek Outside lid straps)
  sell that same strap path **without** the lid as top compression. The path is the anchor; the lid rides on it.
- Exo and Stone Glacier cinch at the **rear (frame side)** with ladder locks and clip at the front. Exo's ladder-lock
  choice is about play: buckles let the lid move.
- Lids are 8–11 L (500–650 cu in) and 170–230 g (6–8.1 oz) in this class. They count outside the main bag's volume.
- The field failure to design against is re-attaching the top over a full shelf (Exo). A top anchor that stays
  attached to the frame when the lid opens, and that does not need to reach over meat, avoids it.
