# Design language research: what makes a premium mountain-hunting pack read as high-end (BL-063, iteration 1)

Date: 2026-11-24. Ticket: `docs/backlog/BL-063-iter1-looks.md`. Owner rubric: `docs/RUBRIC.md` axis 3 ("looks better").
Used by: `designs/pack_5200/iter1/looks/design_language.md` (the brief) and the A/B/C concepts next to it.

Scope and rule. This file pulls **principles** out of public product pages, reviews and design writing. It does not
describe any brand's trade dress in a way meant to be copied: no logos, names, camo patterns, signature colour
pairings or signature panel shapes are taken over. Where a product is named it is as evidence that a principle exists
in the market, not as a model to imitate (CLAUDE.md hard rule "Original IP only").

Limits. Most hunting-pack reviews judge function, not looks; a search for reviews that judge hunting packs on their
appearance found almost none (S8). The principles below therefore lean on (a) what the premium makers *choose* to
offer (colourways, materials, layouts), which is visible on their product pages, (b) designer interviews from the
broader carry and outdoor trade, and (c) hunter forum threads on colour. Points that rest on our own judgement and
not on a source are marked **JUDGEMENT**.

## 2. Principles

Each principle has the evidence and what it means for CORRIE F.

### P1. Solid first; pattern is the exception, and it is placed (S1, S2, S3, S5)
Premium mountain-hunting makers now lead with solid, muted earth tones; at least one says so as policy (S1), another
offers only two solids (S2), a third adds a two-tone solid that reviewers singled out (S3). Camo survives as one option
in a range, not as the default. **For us:** the house rule (mostly solid, camo as edgy accent panels) is already the
market's premium reading. The question is *where* the camo goes, not whether.

### P2. Two-tone blocking only where it follows the structure (S3, S6, S7)
Two-tone can look "sharp" (S3) or can fight the silhouette (S6). The difference, in our reading (**JUDGEMENT**, from
S6's case): blocking that follows real panel lines and real volumes (a whole side, a whole base, the whole top)
reinforces the shape; blocking that is cut across panels as decoration, or in many small pieces, breaks it up.
Colour is the last pass that "brings the look together" (S7), so it must be designed against the panel map, not
painted on after.

### P3. Few accent pieces, large enough to read (S6, S11, **JUDGEMENT**)
On a phone-sized render, or at 20 m, a 25 mm camo stripe reads as piping and nine small camo pieces read as confetti.
One or two accent zones, each a whole panel, read as intent. Rams' "as little design as possible" (S11) applies: count
the accent pieces and cut the count.

### P4. Clean exterior = fewer loose straps, shorter tails, tonal webbing (S4, S7, S8)
Reviewers reward strap-free, snag-free exteriors (S8) and complain about loose, floppy lids on long straps (S4).
Narrow webbing allows small hardware (S7). High-contrast black webbing on a mid-tone body draws every strap as a
line across the bag; tonal webbing keeps the strap function and drops the visual noise (**JUDGEMENT**, consistent with
S6's "simplified palette made the bag's lines stand out").

### P5. Hardware is a deliberate, small family (S9, S13)
Premium carry brands treat hardware as a design decision (S9: all-metal as statement). For a hunting pack the quiet,
light answer is the opposite: one acetal family, one colour, matte, smallest rated size. Coloured military-grade 25 mm
hardware exists in Foliage and Coyote (S13), so a tonal hardware option is buyable; an exact olive match is not
confirmed.

### P6. Every seam earns its place, and the seam map is the line language (S12, S11)
The premium technical look comes from seams that sit where the structure or wear needs them, then are aligned so
they read as one system (S12). For us: a colour break should sit on an existing seam or a print boundary, never add a
seam only for looks unless it removes another part (an overlay) or a mass.

### P7. Calm proportion and a resolved top (S10, S4)
The test is whether the proportions "feel calm" (S10). The most common unresolved area on hunting packs is the top:
lids on long straps, stacked buckles, guy lines (S4). A resolved top has one clear cap shape and as few straps as the
function allows.

### P8. The frame is honest but quiet (S2, S11, S14)
External-frame hunting packs show their frame (S2: carbon stays, compression "built into the frame"). Premium reads
when the visible frame faces look intended: consistent fillets, a single rib language, matte finish, no
FE-looking blobs. Rams' "honest" (S11) means we do not hide the frame behind dummy covers (mass) but we do style the
faces we see.

## 3. What we will not do (IP guard)
- No camo other than our own (Owner Base, Cirquefall); no colour named or matched to a competitor's named colourway.
- No copying of a competitor's specific two-tone split (for example a specific lid/body or base/body split shape) or
  of their logo placement. Our splits follow CORRIE F's own pattern pieces (section P2, P6).
- No brand marks of others; our mark is original (brief section 7).

## 4. Open research gaps
- No cited source rates hunting packs on looks with a method; the owner's A/B pick is the real test (RUBRIC axis 3).
- Hardware colour: ITW "Foliage" vs Krummholz Sage / Elk Wallow not measured. Buy a sample card before choosing.
- Printed solid vs dyed nylon colour match (metamerism between a sublimated polyester solid and a dyed nylon 330D)
  is unverified; the camo release's strike-off route (ΔE00 checks) covers it.
